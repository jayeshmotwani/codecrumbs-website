# Checkpoint 0 — Welcome to the GitHub 7-Day Challenge

**Checkpoint:** 0 (Welcome) · **Runtime:** ~2.5 min · **Difficulty:** none — just watch · **Then:** start Day 1 today

**Goal of this video:** get the learner excited, set the one habit that makes people finish, and make it 100% normal to ask questions in the community.

---

[ON CAMERA]

Hey, and welcome — you're in. This is the GitHub 7-Day Challenge, and over the next seven days you're going to go from "I've heard of GitHub" to having a profile that actually looks like a developer's.

Let me be specific about what you'll walk away with, because it's not just "you learned Git." By Day 7 you'll have a real project on GitHub with a proper README, a clean commit history, a couple of branches, your first-ever pull request to someone else's project, and a profile page with your best work pinned and a profile README that introduces you. That last one — that's the link you put on your resume.

Here's how it runs. One checkpoint a day, seven days. Each day is one small skill and takes somewhere between ten and forty minutes. It's built to fit around a job or classes — you do not need a free weekend.

Why one small piece a day? Git technically has hundreds of commands. Working developers use about eight to ten of them, every day, without thinking. We're not going to read the manual. We're going to build muscle memory one command at a time until it's boring — and boring is the goal.

Now, the single most important habit for this challenge: **post your checkpoint every single day in the Challenge Feed.** A screenshot, a link, whatever the day asks for. That's what keeps you accountable, and honestly, people who post on Day 1 are dramatically more likely to still be here on Day 7. So after this video, go do Day 1 and post it. Today.

One more thing, and I mean this. **You are going to get stuck.** A command won't run, an error message will make no sense, a push will get rejected for no obvious reason. That is not a sign that you're bad at this — that is the exact experience every professional developer has every week. The only difference is they ask for help fast instead of fighting it alone for an hour.

So the moment you have a doubt, bring it to the community. Don't wait until you're frustrated. Paste the exact command you typed and the exact error you got, and someone will help you — usually pretty quickly. And please don't worry that your question is too basic. "What does 'origin' actually mean?" is a genuinely great question, and a lot of people are quietly wondering the same thing.

Seven days is short. Show up for twenty minutes a day and you'll finish this.

Day 1 is the easiest one of the whole challenge — it's mostly downloads and clicking, no commands to memorise. If you can install an app, you can do today. Let's get you set up. I'll see you in the Day 1 checkpoint.
